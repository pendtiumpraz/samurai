⚔️ The Blade

Precision Meets Tradition

Every katana begins as raw tamahagane steel, folded over 1,000 times to remove impurities and create the iconic hada grain pattern. Master smiths spend 3 to 6 months forging a single blade.

Our smiths are certified by the Japanese Sword Smith Association, using only the finest materials from the Tatara furnaces of Shimane prefecture — an unbroken 800-year tradition.

⚔️ Sharpness

Cutting Beyond Perception

A true katana does not cut — it passes through. Our blades achieve a 12° edge angle through 40+ hours of manual honing on natural Japanese water stones.

🔨 Craftsmanship

Forged by Fire

The soul of a katana is forged in fire. Tamahagane steel is heated to precisely 1,200°C, then hammered, folded, and quenched in a process refined over centuries.

Differential hardening creates the iconic hamon line — a wavy pattern along the edge that represents the perfect balance between hardness (for sharpness) and flexibility (for resilience).

🔨 The Forge

Born in Flames

Molten steel, hammer and fire. Each blade is shaped by hands that have honed their craft for decades. No machines. No shortcuts.
